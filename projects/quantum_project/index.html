Quantum Thermodynamics

Correlations and work extraction in quantum systems

During my physics degree at the University of Buenos Aires, I worked on the resource theory of quantum thermodynamics under the supervision of Augusto Roncaglia, in collaboration with Federico Cerisola.

Classical thermodynamics describes systems with a huge number of particles, where fluctuations average out and quantities like free energy tell us exactly how much work a process costs or produces. At the nanoscale, this is no longer true: systems are small, processes may happen only once, and fluctuations matter. The resource theory approach treats thermodynamics as a set of allowed operations (exchanging energy with a thermal bath while conserving the total energy) and asks which state transformations are possible and at what cost. In this single-shot regime, the standard laws of thermodynamics are replaced by a family of constraints that are more restrictive than the usual free energy.

Correlations as a resource

Correlations between physical systems are a valuable resource in quantum information, and creating them usually has an energetic cost. We studied their role in the thermodynamic process of state formation, that is, the minimum work needed to prepare a given state from thermal equilibrium. We found that correlations can also be a thermodynamic resource: the cost of preparing many copies of a state is reduced if the copies are allowed to be correlated. We computed this minimum cost for any finite number of subsystems and showed that, by allowing an amount of correlations that grows only logarithmically with the number of subsystems, the standard result where free energy quantifies the cost is recovered in the asymptotic limit. This work was the topic of my physics thesis and was published in Nature Communications.

Heat engines in the nanoregime

We then applied these ideas to heat engines operating in the single-shot regime and extracting a finite amount of deterministic work, that is, work without fluctuations. We showed that the efficiency of these engines is strictly smaller than Carnot’s efficiency, due to a fundamental irreversibility in single-shot transformations. When fluctuations in the extracted work are allowed, there is a trade-off: larger fluctuations give higher efficiency. Finally, when the working medium is made of many particles, creating correlations between them that scale logarithmically with their number allows the engine to approach Carnot’s efficiency while still extracting deterministic work.

References

2022

  1. Heat engines with single-shot deterministic work extraction
    Federico Cerisola, Facundo Sapienza, and Augusto J Roncaglia
    Physical Review E, Aug 2022
    Abs Bib HTML

    We introduce heat engines working in the nanoregime that allow one to extract a finite amount of deterministic work. Using the resource theory approach to themodynamics, we show that the efficiency of these cycles is strictly smaller than Carnot’s, and we associate this difference with a fundamental irreversibility that is present in single-shot transformations. When fluctuations in the extracted work are allowed there is a trade-off between their size and the efficiency. As the size of fluctuations increases so does the efficiency and optimal efficiency is attained for unbounded fluctuations, while a certain amount of deterministic work is drawn from the cycle. Finally, we show that when the working medium is composed of many particles, by creating an amount of correlations between the subsystems that scale logarithmically with their number, Carnot’s efficiency can also be approached in the asymptotic limit along with deterministic work extraction.

2019

  1. Correlations as a resource in quantum thermodynamics
    Facundo Sapienza, Federico Cerisola, and Augusto J Roncaglia
    Nature communications, Aug 2019
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    The presence of correlations in physical systems can be a valuable resource for many quantum information tasks. They are also relevant in thermodynamic transformations, and their creation is usually associated to some energetic cost. In this work, we study the role of correlations in the thermodynamic process of state formation in the single-shot regime, and find that correlations can also be viewed as a resource. First, we show that the energetic cost of creating multiple copies of a given state can be reduced by allowing correlations in the final state. We obtain the minimum cost for every finite number of subsystems, and then we show that this feature is not restricted to the case of copies. More generally, we demonstrate that in the asymptotic limit, by allowing a logarithmic amount of correlations, we can recover standard results where the free energy quantifies this minimum cost.
