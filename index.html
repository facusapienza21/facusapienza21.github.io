I am currently a Chair of the Multidisciplinary Institute in Artificial Intelligence at the Institute of Environmental Geosciences (IGE), Université Grenoble Alpes (France), and a member of the ODINN group. Before this, I completed my PhD in Statistics at UC Berkeley, was a postdoctoral researcher at the Stanford Icy Physics Lab, and a visiting professor at the Department of Mathematics at the University of Buenos Aires.

My research combines differential equations and machine learning to model complex geophysical systems. Using differentiable programming, we can train models whose output is the numerical solution of a physical differential equation, combining data-driven and physics-driven approaches. I apply these methods to glaciology, where we learn the laws governing the flow of ice and model glaciers at global scale, and to paleomagnetism and geomagnetism, where we learn from scarce and noisy data on the sphere about the past motion of tectonic plates and the evolution of Earth’s magnetic field. I am also enthusiastic about open science, reproducible research and teaching. Learn more in Projects.

🌱 I am recruiting! I have open PhD and postdoc positions in my group in Grenoble, including a PhD on physics-informed machine learning for Andean glaciers. Feel free to reach out if you are interested!
